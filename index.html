Rogge Group

We probe quantum matter and build quantum interfaces at the atomic scale — using ultralow-temperature STM to uncover emergent phenomena in quantum materials, developing erbium emitters in silicon for telecom-wavelength quantum networking, and manufacturing superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors for optical quantum information processing.

Research

Erbium Emitters in Si

Studying the spin and optical properties of erbium implanted in silicon, with the goal of integrating single Er ions into nanophotonic cavities for quantum networking and transduction at telecom wavelengths.

Superconducting Nanowire Single Photon Detectors

Developing and manufacturing custom SNSPDs — the world's most sensitive photon detectors — operating at telecom wavelengths with near-unity efficiency, low dark counts, and high count rates.

Scanning Tunnelling Microscopy

Ultralow-temperature STM with high-frequency and optical capabilities — probing loss mechanisms in superconducting films, correlated states in disordered systems, and spatially precise single electron spin resonance.

Donor Atom Qubits

Using scanning tunnelling microscopy and spectroscopy to place and probe individual donor atoms in silicon with atomic precision — the building blocks of a phosphorus-in-silicon quantum processor.

Group Leader

Scientia Professor
Sven Rogge

Sven Rogge is a Scientia Professor at UNSW Sydney and Dean of the Faculty of Science. He leads the Silicon Qubit Environment and Interface program within the ARC Centre of Excellence for Quantum Computer Performance and Integration (ARC QPI). His group brings together experimental and theoretical approaches to understand how qubits interact with the silicon environment — work that is central to the push toward scalable silicon quantum computers.

Before joining UNSW in 2011, Sven held positions at the Kavli Institute for Quantum Nanoscience at Delft University and at Stanford University.
